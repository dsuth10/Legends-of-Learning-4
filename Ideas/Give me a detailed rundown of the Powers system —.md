# Give me a detailed rundown of the Powers system — special abilities unlocked by leveling up that correspond to real-world classroom privileges (e.g., eating a snack, taking a break plus a broad range of example powers that could be used

Here is a detailed breakdown of the Classcraft **Powers system** — one of the platform's most popular and motivating features.

***

## How the Powers System Works

Powers are real-world classroom privileges that students unlock by leveling up through earning XP (Experience Points). Each time a student accumulates enough XP to level up, they earn **1 Power Point (PP)**, which they spend to learn a new power from their character class's **power tree**. Once a power is learned, it can be used repeatedly for the rest of the game — as long as the student has enough **Action Points (AP)** to activate it. Each power costs a set number of AP to use, so students must manage their resources strategically.[^1]

***

## The Power Tree Structure

Powers are organized into **three tiers** per character class — Tier 1 (basic), Tier 2 (intermediate), and Tier 3 (advanced). Higher-tier powers require lower-tier powers to be learned first as prerequisites, encouraging consistent engagement over time. This progression means younger or less engaged students still access fun low-cost perks, while motivated students can work toward major rewards.[^1]

***

## The Three Character Classes \& Their Powers

Each class has a distinct power theme that shapes their role in the classroom team.[^2]

### Guardian (Warrior)

Guardians are the protectors — their powers tend to focus on **shielding teammates from consequences** and personal academic advantages.[^3][^4]

- **Protect** — prevents a teammate from losing HP from a penalty event
- **Fortify** — absorbs incoming HP damage for the whole team
- **Frontal Assault** *(teacher-customisable)* — all team members get a homework pass
- **Secret Weapon** *(teacher-customisable)* — during an assignment, the Guardian can have half the answers revealed[^3]
- **Change your seat** — move to any available seat in the room
- **Choose not to go first** for presentations or performances
- **Avoid light damage** — immune to small HP penalties under a set threshold[^3]


### Healer

Healers keep the team alive — their powers focus on **restoring HP to teammates** and offering academic lifelines.[^1][^3]

- **Heal 1 / Heal 2 / Heal 3** — restore 10, 20, or 30 HP to a teammate[^3]
- **Healing Circle** — all team members (except the Healer) gain 15 HP[^3]
- **Revive** — when a teammate falls to 0 HP, they avoid all penalties and return with 1 HP[^3]
- **Prayer** *(default Classcraft power)* — Healer can access their notes during a test[^1]
- **Ardent Faith** *(teacher-customisable)* — re-submit an assignment for a new grade[^3]
- **Favor of the Gods** — pass on a bellwork/starter activity for full credit[^3]
- **Sainthood** — receive a small treat/gift from the teacher[^3]
- **Extra Credit for Team** — unlocks an extra credit assignment for the whole team[^3]


### Mage

Mages are powerhouses focused on **boosting AP for teammates** and earning high-impact individual freedoms.[^1][^3]

- **Mana Transfer** — all teammates except Mages gain 7 AP[^1][^3]
- **Teleport** *(default)* — leave the classroom for up to 2 minutes (e.g., hall pass, pick up materials)[^1]
- **Invisibility** *(default)* — exempt from being called on in class for a day[^3]
- **Mana Shield** — prevent personal HP loss (costs 3 AP per 1 HP saved)[^3]
- **Cheat Death** — a fallen teammate pre-rolls the cursed die and can accept the better outcome[^3]
- **Time Warp** — allows an additional Daily Event (random game event) to be played[^3]
- **Clairvoyance** *(teacher-customisable)* — conjures a refreshment table; everyone on the team gets a piece of candy/snack[^3]
- **Mage Circle** — all team members can go to the library or hallway for group work[^3]
- **Fountain of Mana** — fully replenishes one non-Mage teammate's AP[^3]

***

## Broad Range of Teacher-Customisable Powers

One of the system's greatest strengths is that **teachers can fully customise** the real-world meaning of each power. Here is a broad range of examples used by teachers across grade levels:[^5]

**Comfort \& Lifestyle Privileges**

- Eat a snack or drink in class[^5][^3]
- Chew gum in class
- Wear headphones/listen to music while working
- Sit anywhere in the room or bring your own chair
- Work outside or in the hallway[^3]
- Use a standing desk or floor cushion

**Academic Privileges**

- Use notes during a quiz or test[^1]
- Turn in one assignment up to a day late[^3]
- Re-submit an assignment for regrading[^3]
- Skip a bellwork/starter task for full credit[^3]
- Create a bonus question on a quiz[^3]
- Have half the answers on an assignment revealed[^3]
- Skip one homework assignment entirely

**Social \& Classroom Influence**

- Choose your own group for a project
- Choose not to present first[^3]
- Be exempt from being called on randomly for one class[^3]
- Choose a classmate to receive a reward
- Prevent another team from using a power during a Boss Battle (quiz)[^3]

**Teacher Interaction Rewards**

- Receive a small gift from the teacher[^3]
- Earn a positive phone call/note home
- Choose a class activity or game for end of lesson
- Name a character/NPC in the next Boss Battle

**Responsibility \& Leadership Perks**

- Take the class pet home for the weekend
- Be classroom helper or line leader for a day
- Free choice reading or early finisher activity
- Extra time in the library or makerspace

***

## Key Design Principles

The power system is intentionally **cooperative, not just individual** — many of the best powers require spending AP to benefit *teammates*, reinforcing prosocial behaviour. Teachers are encouraged to balance powers so that competitive powers between students don't harm grades or fairness, but instead create fun strategic moments (e.g., blocking another team's power in a boss fight). The system essentially replaces traditional token economies with a far more engaging, narrative-driven alternative.[^2][^3]
