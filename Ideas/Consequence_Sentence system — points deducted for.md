# Consequence/Sentence system — points deducted for inappropriate behavior; in-game losses map to real-world consequences (e.g., clean-up duty

Here is a full detailed breakdown of the Classcraft **Consequence/Sentence System**.

***

## How It Works: HP as a Behaviour Gauge

Every student's character has a pool of **Health Points (HP)** that acts as a real-time behaviour gauge. When a student behaves negatively — arriving late, talking over the teacher, having a phone out, forgetting materials — the teacher deducts HP from that student's character in the app. The amount deducted depends on the severity of the behaviour, and teachers configure all of this themselves. Different character classes have different maximum HP, so Guardians are naturally more resilient than Mages, adding a strategic dimension to class selection.[^1][^2][^3]

***

## Falling in Battle

When a student's HP reaches **zero**, they "fall in battle" — a dramatic in-game moment that triggers the consequence process. At this point, **teammates have a brief window to save the fallen student** using their powers before a consequence is applied:[^3]

- A **Healer** can use *Revive* — the fallen student avoids all penalties and returns with 1 HP[^4]
- A **Guardian** can use *Protect* — absorbs incoming HP damage before the student falls[^4]
- A **Mage** can use *Cheat Death* — the fallen student rolls the Cursed Die twice and takes the better outcome[^4]

If no teammate saves them, the student must **roll the Cursed Die**.[^5]

***

## The Cursed Die

The Cursed Die is a virtual six-sided die (rolled on screen in the app) that randomly assigns one of **six teacher-configured sentences**. The outcome is entirely random, which adds an element of suspense and removes the teacher from being the direct "punisher" — the game mechanics deliver the consequence instead. This is one of the most psychologically clever aspects of the system: students often accept random consequences from a game more willingly than a direct teacher reprimand.[^6][^5]

One of the six faces can be set to **"Nothing"** — meaning a lucky roll results in no consequence at all, which students love and fear in equal measure.[^6]

After rolling, the student is **brought back into the game with 1 HP**, but all of their **teammates lose 10 HP** as a ripple effect. If a teammate then falls to 0 HP as a result, their remaining teammates lose another 10 HP — creating a **cascading effect** that resolves itself once everyone has taken their penalty.[^3]

***

## Customising the Six Sentences

Teachers fully customise all six faces of the Cursed Die to suit their classroom, age group, and school policies. Here is a broad range of real-world examples used by teachers:[^6]

**Academic Consequences**

- Submit your next assignment one day early[^6]
- Redo a piece of work that was below standard
- Complete a short reflection or self-assessment task
- Write a brief apology or "what I'll do differently" note
- Sit a short bonus quiz on today's content
- Complete an additional set of practice questions

**Classroom Duty Consequences**

- Clean up duty at the end of class[^7]
- Stack chairs or tidy the classroom at the end of the lesson
- Wipe down the whiteboard
- Collect and distribute materials for the class
- Be responsible for returning class equipment

**Behavioural Reflection Consequences**

- Sit in a designated "reflection seat" for 10 minutes
- Write three classroom rules and why they matter
- Have a brief one-on-one check-in with the teacher after class
- Complete a behaviour reflection slip

**Social/Fun Consequences** *(used to keep tone light, especially in younger grades)*

- Perform a silly task (e.g., do 10 jumping jacks, tell a joke to the class)
- Answer a random trivia question in front of the class[^1]
- Bring a treat or snack for the class next lesson[^1]
- Sit in the "throne of shame" (a designated funny chair) for five minutes

**The "Nothing" Face**

- Lucky escape — no consequence applied[^6]

***

## Team Consequence Mechanic

The cascading team HP loss is a core part of the design philosophy — it means **peer accountability is built into the system**. Students are naturally motivated to keep teammates out of trouble because one person falling in battle hurts the whole team. This turns behaviour management into a collective responsibility rather than a teacher-vs-student dynamic.[^8][^3]

***

## Negative Behaviour Examples Teachers Typically Assign HP Damage For

| Behaviour | Typical HP Deduction |
| :-- | :-- |
| Phone out without permission [^1] | 5–10 HP |
| Talking over the teacher [^2] | 5–10 HP |
| Arriving late to class [^2] | 10–15 HP |
| Forgetting materials/instrument [^2] | 5–10 HP |
| Submitting work late [^9] | 10–20 HP |
| Being rude to a classmate [^9] | 15–20 HP |
| Device not charged [^1] | 5 HP |
| Off-task during work time | 5–10 HP |

The exact values are fully teacher-configurable, so they can be calibrated to match the severity of infractions and the overall HP pool of each character class.[^2]

***

## Why It Works Pedagogically

The system reframes discipline as a **game mechanic** rather than a personal confrontation. The randomness of the Cursed Die creates excitement and neutrality — students often laugh at their sentence rather than resent it — and the team-saving mechanic builds genuine prosocial motivation to help peers behave well. For PBIS (Positive Behavioural Interventions and Supports) aligned schools, the HP/consequence system pairs directly with existing tiered behaviour frameworks.[^10][^8][^3][^6]
